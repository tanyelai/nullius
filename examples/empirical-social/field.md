# Field: quantitative social science

## What counts as evidence

An identification strategy, stated before the estimate. A coefficient is evidence for a causal
claim only through the design that makes it one, and the design is what a reviewer reads.

Where the design is observational, the claim the paper may make is about association unless the
assumptions for identification are named and defended one by one.

## Normal scale

Power, not n. A study should say what effect size it could have detected, and a null result
without that number says nothing. Cluster the standard errors at the level treatment was
assigned, and say which level that was.

## Standard baselines

The specification everyone in this literature runs, plus the two that would break the result if
the effect were an artefact. A single specification is a result nobody can weigh.

## Standard reporting

Pre-registration where the design allows it, and where it does not, an explicit statement that
the analysis is exploratory. Report the full specification curve, or say why not.

## Known traps

- **Researcher degrees of freedom.** The analysis decision taken after seeing the data is not
  dishonest and is not confirmatory either; the only fix is ordering, and the only evidence of
  ordering is a record made before.
- **The file drawer.** A literature of published positives is not a literature.
- **Measurement passed off as construct.** A survey item is not the attitude.
- **The subgroup found afterwards.**
- **Generalising from one country, one platform or one year without saying so.**

## Words that mean two things

`significant`, `bias`, `robustness check`. All three carry a technical and a colloquial sense
in the same paragraph regularly.
