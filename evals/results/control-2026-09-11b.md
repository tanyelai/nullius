Same three arms, same conditions, one harder task. Run one's own weakness section said the
topic was too easy and named what a harder one would need: a literature the model has not

**Task, identical in all three arms.** *Do sparse autoencoder features found in language

is the answer?* About 350 words.

## Three things this run says that the first did not

**1. The credited-name gate separated the arms.** Arm A credited *Templeton et al., Scaling
Monosemanticity*, *Ameisen et al., Circuit Tracing*, *Lindsey et al., On the Biology of a
Large Language Model* and *Leask et al.* by name and title with no identifier beside any of
them, in a note that carried ten resolvable identifiers elsewhere. So this is not the
bibliography-invisible case the row is hedged for: a reader cannot resolve four of the works
that note leans on. Arm B did it once. Arm C did not do it, because `check` refuses a
credited surname with no resolved work behind it.

**2. The advice arm was the most verbose, and by a distance.** Against a task asking for
about 350 words, the arm with no standards at all overshot by 15%, the arm holding a declared
400-word budget came in at 3%, and the arm that had just read 1,933 words of standards --
including *lead with the answer, then stop* and *short is the default and long is a request*
-- overshot by **81%**.

The most likely reading is uncomfortable and worth stating: **advice about thoroughness
produces thoroughness performance.** `invariants/*.md` spends most of its words on warrant,
status, independence and counter-search, so an arm that has just read it writes more in order
to be seen to have done all of that. The instruction telling it to be brief lost to the
instructions implying it should be complete. That is section 3's own thesis landing on
section 3's own instrument.

**3. Still zero fabricated identifiers, in six arms across two runs.** Including three arms
with no harness at all, on a topic chosen because the identifiers should not be memorised.
The failure that the citation gate exists to prevent, and that
[REFERENCES.md](../../REFERENCES.md) cites three papers about, has not once occurred in this
experiment.

## What did not separate the arms

All three answered the actual question correctly and with appropriate hedging: *not settled*,
*contested*, *contested*. The status axis did not change anyone's conclusion here. Arm C
recorded ten claims, nine `single-result`, one `emerging` and one `contested`, and zero
`established` -- the independence arithmetic would not have supported one -- but arms A and B
reached the same verdict in prose without it.

Arm C also opened with the word *Contested* and closed with a section headed *What this note
does not cover*, neither of which was asked for.

## What this run does not establish

Everything run one's last section said, unchanged: n = 1 per topic, arm C had the CLI's
write-time refusals and not the Stop gate, and the experiment was designed by the author of
the thing under test. Two runs is two, and the length result rests on a single number per arm.

## The honest reading, updated

Run one said advice was enough for the prose and the gates bought the ledger. On a harder
topic that is too generous to advice. Advice produced the least readable artifact of the
three and left one credited name unresolvable; the bare arm left four. What the gates bought
was a draft that hit its length, credited nobody it could not back, and left behind ten
claims a third party can re-check.

None of which is the thing the citation gate was built for, and that gate has now had nothing
to catch twice.
