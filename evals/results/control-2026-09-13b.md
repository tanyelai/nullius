# control, fifth run · 2026-09-13

The fourth run was on a mature literature: forty years of refereed work, and both arms produced
a defensible answer. The difference it showed was checkability rather than correctness, which
is a real difference and a quiet one. This run picks the opposite kind of subject on purpose --
new, preprint-dominated, and with a popular account running well ahead of the evidence -- to
see whether the difference gets louder.

It does.

**Task, in both arms.** *Do large reasoning models actually reason, or do they pattern-match?
How settled is the answer? Do the literature search properly before you answer. About 350 words
in answer.md.*

Same launch as [the fourth run](control-2026-09-13.md): `--setting-sources ''` for the arm with
nothing, a `nullius init --field "machine learning"` project for the arm with the harness,
neither prompt naming the tool. Both processes were allowed to exit before anything was read,
which is the rule the fourth run earned.

## 1. The paper at the centre of the dispute, cited through a vendor's blog

This is the finding, and it is one line.

The arm with no harness introduces the key result as *Apple's "The Illusion of Thinking"* and
links it to **`arize.com/blog/the-illusion-of-thinking-what-the-apple-ai-paper-says-about-llm-reasoning/`**
-- a monitoring company's marketing post *about* the paper.

The arm with the harness holds the same work as `shojaee2025`, resolved through the indexes to
**Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems**, carrying a DOI, flagged peer-reviewed,
read to `method` depth, with a passage checked verbatim against the cached full text.

One session routed a NeurIPS paper through a vendor blog. The other could not, because
`nullius cite` resolves a work or refuses it, and a blog post is not a work.

## 2. Five of ten links are not papers

Counted, not characterised:

| what the link points at | count |
|---|---|
| arXiv preprints | 5 |
| a vendor's blog post | 1 |
| a conference **poster page**, not the paper | 1 |
| a company's own publication venue | 1 |
| a benchmark organisation's product page | 1 |
| a Substack | 1 |

**Zero DOIs in the document.** Zero works presented as peer-reviewed. The single strongest
claim in it -- that frontier systems have produced novel mathematics -- rests on the benchmark
organisation's page, with no paper behind it anywhere in the text.

None of that makes the answer wrong. It makes every sentence in it unresolvable by the reader
without repeating the search, on a question where half the literature is three months old.

## 3. What the peer-review flag actually did

Nine of the harness arm's eighteen references are not peer reviewed, and the ledger says so on
every one: eight arXiv, one Research Square. That is not a defect of the search, it is the
shape of this literature, and it is the thing the popular account of this debate never states.

It changed the prose. The harness arm writes *"Lawsen (2025, preprint)"* and *"Shojaee et al.
(2025, NeurIPS)"* and *"Webb et al. (2025, PNAS Nexus)"* -- the status of each source travels
with it in the sentence. The arm with no harness names no venue at all for any source, and its
only venue-like signal is the domain in a URL the reader has to hover over.

## 4. The independence arithmetic permitted something, for the first time

Across four earlier runs no claim ever reached `established`; the author-group arithmetic never
supported one. Here one did:

```
[measured | established | holds]  mirzadeh2024, wu2024
  Frontier LLM accuracy on reasoning benchmarks is not invariant to changes that do not
  alter the underlying problem ...
```

Two independent groups, both measuring, so the status is earned rather than chosen. And the
contested heart of the dispute is recorded as exactly that:

```
[measured | contested | reports]  shojaee2025, lawsen2025
  The reported complete accuracy collapse ... is contested: the same puzzle results are
  attributed by one group to reasoning limits and by another to experimental design ...
```

A gate that only ever refuses is a gate nobody can calibrate. This run is the first where the
same arithmetic said yes.

## 5. Five quotes, five checks

The harness arm's answer carries five quoted passages. Run against the cached full texts:

```
nullius: verbatim in mirzadeh2024.
nullius: verbatim in wu2024.
nullius: verbatim in shojaee2025.
nullius: verbatim in lawsen2025.
nullius: verbatim in nikankin2024.
```

Five for five, and the five sources with cached text are exactly the five read to `method`
depth. The other nine are at `abstract` and four were never opened, and the ledger says which
is which.

The arm with no harness quotes nothing and retains nothing to quote against. That is not a
criticism of it -- it is the asymmetry. One of these documents can be audited by a third party
and the other has to be trusted.

## What this run does not establish

One run per arm, one task. The harness arm wrote author-year prose this time rather than the
citekeys it used in the fourth run, so `audit` scores its artifact at **0 identifiers and 5
credited names with nothing behind them** -- for a document every one of whose sources is
resolved. The instrument reads the artifact alone by design, and the fourth run already said
this; what is new is that the score now also depends on which citation style the session
happened to choose.

And the arm with no harness scored **3 identifiers, 0 resolved, 3 could not be checked**,
because OpenAlex was rate limiting this session when the audit ran. *Could not be checked* is
the honest third bucket and not a mark against that arm.

## The honest reading

On a mature literature the two arms differed in what a reader could verify. On this one they
differ in what a reader could *find*: one document's central citation leads to a marketing
post, and the other's leads to NeurIPS. The harness did not make the second session smarter.
It made a blog post an unusable citation, which on a question this young is most of the job.
