# Settled, or merely proposed

Warrant asks *how do you know this*. Status asks *how settled is this in the field*. A claim
can be perfectly warranted and barely established; that cell is the dangerous one.

| status | what earns it | how it may be written |
|---|---|---|
| `textbook` | graduate texts treat it as background | bare assertion |
| `established` | independent replication, disjoint author sets | bare assertion, review cited |
| `contested` | serious work on both sides, both logged | **must name both sides** |
| `emerging` | one or two groups, recent | "early evidence suggests", groups counted |
| `single-result` | one paper, one setting | **attributed and situated, never bare** |
| `folklore` | widely repeated, and you could not reach data | marked unsourced, or dropped |

**The failure that costs years is citing something true but unsettled as though it were
settled**, and building on it.

**Status is capped by source independence**, computed from author identifiers. Two papers
sharing an author cannot produce `established`.

**Pointed the other way, it keeps your open question open.** When the evidence is thin, thin
is the finding, not a gap to paper over with a more assured sentence.
