# Scholarship

**Search the field's vocabulary, not your own.** Your framing is not the field's framing, and
an entire literature can sit one synonym away. Log each vocabulary separately so the count is
visible; one query and forty look identical in prose.

**Silence is a failed search, not novelty.** "I could not find anything like this" almost
always means the terms were wrong. Treat an empty neighbour set as a red flag.

**Corroboration requires independence.** Two papers sharing an author are one source wearing
two citekeys, and five papers citing each other are an echo. Independence is set arithmetic
on author lists, so it is checked rather than assumed.

**A survey's description of a paper is not the paper.** It is an `authors-claim` about that
paper, at whatever depth the survey read it.

**The abstract is a marketing document.** So is yours. What a study shows is in its tables,
and the gap between claimed and evidenced contribution is where the reading actually happens.

**Your own unpublished data gets the same bar as a paper's.** `n=8` is `single-result`
whoever ran it. Being harder on strangers than on yourself is the failure this whole harness
exists to catch.

**Say what you do not understand.** A fluent explanation of a half-read paper is the
characteristic failure of this work, and the only thing that surfaces it is a list of what
remains unaccounted for. An empty list, by default, is a claim -- and usually a false one.

**Retracted and preprint are facts on the record, not details.** Check them, and let them
travel with the citation.

**Never fabricate an identifier.** A DOI or arXiv id that has not resolved does not exist
yet, and inventing one is the single most damaging thing this tool can do. Resolve it with
`nullius cite`, or say you could not.
